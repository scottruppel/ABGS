# Limited Theory & Game Mechanics

## Limited Formats Overview

Limited is a category of Magic formats where players build decks from a restricted, unknown card pool. This contrasts with Constructed formats where players build decks in advance from any legal cards available.

### Why Limited Matters

- **Accessibility**: Players don't need a massive collection to compete
- **Skill-Intensive**: Card evaluation, mana management, and on-the-fly decision-making separate skilled players from casual ones
- **Format Health**: Limited formats are less susceptible to "solved" metagames since each draft is unique
- **Prevents Gaming**: Since the card pool is random and unknown, players can't pre-build decks to exploit specific weaknesses

## The Three Sanctioned Limited Formats

### Booster Draft

**Standard draft format**, most commonly played:

- 8 players (most common)
- Each player opens 3 booster packs
- Pick and pass mechanics (left-right-left pattern)
- 40-card minimum deck
- Most common in competitive play and Magic Online
- Most similar to what players practice

**Variants:**

- Quick Draft (Magic Arena): Draft against bots
- Premier Draft (Magic Arena): Draft and play with real people
- Traditional Draft (Magic Arena): Best of 3 with real opponents

### Sealed Deck

- Each player receives 6 booster packs
- No passing; build the best 40-card deck from your 6 packs
- Simpler than draft but less skill-based
- Uses the same card pool evaluation but less strategy around passing
- Common at PRE (Prerelease) events

### Rochester Draft

- Older variant rarely played competitively
- Packs are opened one at a time and laid out face-up
- Players pick in open information (everyone sees what's available)
- Takes significantly longer than regular draft
- Mentioned for historical context

## Card Pool Composition

### Standard Booster Pack Contents (typically)

- 15 cards total per booster
- 1 rare or mythic rare
- 3 uncommons
- 10 commons
- 1 land (usually a basic land, sometimes a special land)

**Three-pack draft yields approximately 45 cards**, which you'll cut to 40 (23 nonlands + 17 lands).

### Rarity Distribution

- **Mythic Rare (MR)**: Rarest, most powerful, usually the "bomb"
- **Rare (R)**: Powerful, unique effects
- **Uncommon (U)**: Bread and butter of decks, often format-defining
- **Common (C)**: Filler and support cards

In limited, **uncommons often matter more than rares** because they're more abundant and define the format's playable strategies.

## Evaluating Card Power in Limited

### The Bomb Test

A card is a "bomb" if it's likely to win the game by itself. Examples:

- A 7-mana creature that costs 7 and is 7/7 (without evasion) is NOT a bomb
- A 5-mana creature that draws 2 cards and has flying IS a bomb
- A removal spell that kills any creature is better than a creature itself (usually)

Bombs justify off-color picks or bad mana early in packs 2 and 3.

### Efficiency

How much value does a card give you per mana spent?

- **Well-costed**: A 2-mana creature with 2 power and 2 toughness is "on curve"
- **Overcosted**: A 5-mana creature with 3 power and 3 toughness is bad value
- **Efficient**: A 2-mana creature with 3 power and 2 toughness is excellent

### Role in Your Deck

Not all good cards fit all decks:

- An aggressive deck doesn't want 5-mana cards (even good ones)
- A control deck doesn't want creatures that don't impact the board
- A green deck wants mana acceleration; red deck wants fast creatures

## Mana Management in Limited

### Mana Bases

Limited mana bases are usually basic lands with minimal fixing:

- **Two-color deck**: 8-9 of one basic land type, 8-9 of another = 17 lands total
- **Two-color with light splash**: 9 of primary 1, 8 of primary 2, 4-5 basics of splash color + 1-2 fixing lands
- **Three-color deck**: Requires careful fixing; usually only playable if you have good dual lands

### Mana Curve

Your mana curve is the distribution of mana costs:

```
Mana Cost:  1   2   3   4   5   6+
Typical:    1-2 4-5 4-5 3-4 2-3 1-2
```

- You need plays for early turns
- Most of your playables should be in the 2-4 mana range
- Late-game payoffs should be limited

### Mana Flooding vs. Screw

- **Mana flooding**: Drawing too many lands, not enough spells
- **Mana screw**: Drawing too few lands or lands of the wrong color

Limited decks are built to minimize both through proper land counts and mana curve.

## Creature Combat in Limited

### Creature Roles

**Evasive creatures** (flying, unblockable):
- More valuable in limited
- Harder to block
- Often race faster than ground creatures

**Efficiency creatures** (good stats for cost):
- The backbone of most decks
- Form the early game

**Bomb creatures** (unique effects):
- Likely to win games
- Often expensive but warrant the mana

### Combat Tricks and Evasion

Cards that affect combat:

- **+1/+1 pump**: Tricks your opponent during combat
- **Bounce effects**: Return a creature to hand
- **Protection**: Grant hexproof, protection from color, or other evasion
- **First strike**: Trade efficiently in combat

These are extremely valuable in limited because they create surprises.

## Removal and Interaction

### Types of Removal

**Creature Removal:**
- Kill spells (permanent removal)
- Bounce spells (temporary removal)
- Damage spells
- Discard (sometimes)

**Permanents:**
- Destroy enchantments or artifacts
- Exile effects (vs. returning to hand)

**Direct Damage:**
- Burns the opponent directly
- Usually weaker than creature removal

### Why Removal Matters

In limited, removal is **extremely valuable** because:

- It answers threats without requiring a favorable trade
- It's harder to dodge than combat
- Limited creatures are often efficient, so trading in combat requires good creatures of your own

A format with limited removal tends to be creature-heavy and aggressive.

## Synergy and Mechanics

### Mechanical Synergies

Limited formats often have mechanics that create synergies:

- **Sacrifice outlets** + creatures with "dies" triggers
- **Threshold mechanics** (conditions like having 7+ cards in graveyard)
- **Tribal synergies** (creatures that reward you for playing creatures of a type)
- **Spell velocity** (casting many spells triggers effects)

These synergies define the format's archetypes.

### Mana Ramp

Green's primary mechanic:

- Mana dorks (creatures that tap for mana)
- Land ramp (search your deck for lands)
- Artifact ramp (stones and talismans)

Ramp allows you to cast expensive spells ahead of schedule.

### Card Draw

Typically the best "fair" effect:

- Drawing two cards for X mana
- Creatures that draw cards when they enter or attack
- Generally, blue's advantage

## Statistical Concepts in Limited

### Win Rate by Color

All colors should be roughly balanced:

- No color should win >55% or lose <45% of games
- Indicates power level is appropriate
- Deviations suggest mechanic problems or too much synergy

### Playable Density

How many playable cards are in the format?

- **High density** (>50% of cards playable): Many viable strategies
- **Low density** (<40% of cards playable): Narrow archetype focus

### Grade Distribution

- A cards: Bombs, powerful, high pick priority
- B cards: Solid, efficient, good in any deck
- C cards: Playable, context-dependent
- D cards: Situational, rarely playable
- F cards: Unplayable

## Deck Archetypes and Meta

### Archetype Tiers

**Tier 1**: Strong, consistent, well-supported
**Tier 2**: Viable but requires good synergy to work
**Tier 3**: Weak, only works with exceptional cards

### Open Lanes

A "lane" is an archetype with few players drafting it. If you're in an open lane:

- You get premium picks later in packs
- You can assemble a cohesive strategy
- Your deck is likely stronger than one built fighting over contested colors

### Forced Metas

Sometimes the card pool forces players into specific archetypes:

- Limited removal = creature-heavy format
- Lots of flying = evasion is prioritized
- Mana ramp present = bigger cards are prioritized

## Limited vs. Constructed

### Key Differences

| Aspect | Limited | Constructed |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Card Pool** | Restricted, random | Full, known |
| **Deck Building** | On the fly | Pre-built |
| **Synergy** | Incidental | Carefully planned |
| **Skill** | Evaluation + drafting | Metagame knowledge + play skill |
| **Format Health** | Generally healthy | Susceptible to "solved" metas |
| **Power Level** | Consistent | Can vary wildly |

### Why Limited is Harder

1. **Unknown information**: You don't know what's coming
2. **Quick decisions**: You have limited time to pick cards
3. **Evaluation**: You must instantly assess card value
4. **Adaptation**: You must pivot strategies based on signals
