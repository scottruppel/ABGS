# Magic: The Gathering Draft - Fundamentals & Strategy

## What is Draft in MTG?

Draft is a Limited format in Magic: The Gathering. Unlike Constructed formats where players build decks in advance from their entire card collection, Limited requires players to build decks from a restricted pool of cards obtained during the draft itself.

Draft is played with a group of players (typically 8) seated around a table. Each player opens a booster pack, selects one card and passes the remaining cards to the player on their left. This process continues until all cards from the pack are distributed. The process repeats for a second and third booster pack, with the second pack passing to the right and the third pack returning to the left.

## The Draft Process

### The Rules of Drafting

1. **Pack Opening**: Each player opens one booster pack, removes tokens and non-playable cards.

2. **Pick and Pass**: 
   - Pack 1: Pick a card, pass remaining cards to the left
   - Pack 2: Pick a card, pass remaining cards to the right
   - Pack 3: Pick a card, pass remaining cards to the left

3. **Deck Building**: Once all three packs are drafted, players build a 40-card minimum deck from their drafted cards plus as many basic lands as they choose.

4. **Deck Size**: Draft decks have a minimum of 40 cards. You cannot go below 40.

5. **Land Ratio**: Generally 17 lands and 23 nonlands. More aggressive decks may run 16 lands; slower decks may run 18 lands.

6. **Creature/Spell Ratio**: Generally 13-18 creatures with 5-10 noncreature spells, though this is highly flexible based on the format's mechanics and archetype.

7. **Sideboard**: All drafted cards not used in the main deck function as a sideboard for game 2 and 3 (in Best of 3 formats).

## Core Draft Strategy Principles

### 1. Start with Powerful Cards

At the start of Pack 1, prioritize the most powerful card in each pack. Take bombs (high-impact cards that win games on their own), efficient removal, or strong creatures. Don't worry about color commitment early—just take power.

### 2. Stay Open

In the first 5-6 picks of Pack 1, avoid committing too early to a specific color or archetype. Strange things happen in draft, and you want flexibility to respond to the signals you receive from both the packs you open and the cards being passed to you.

### 3. Read the Signals

As you pick, pay attention to which colors are being passed to you (cards that are coming from your left neighbor, which means they're not picking them). If you see quality blue cards coming around, it might signal that blue is open in your seat. Conversely, if you're passing blue cards that are good and they're not coming back, blue might be contested.

### 4. Find Your Lane

By the end of Pack 1 (picks 8-14), you should have a clear direction. This is usually two colors with maybe a splash of a third. Knowing your lane helps you make better decisions in Packs 2 and 3.

### 5. Solidify Your Archetype in Pack 2

Pack 2 is where you lock in your archetype. You should know what synergies exist in the format and pick cards that fit your lane. Pick up good mana fixing if needed, but prioritize playable cards for your colors.

### 6. Patch Holes in Pack 3

In the final pack, address any gaps in your deck. Missing 2-drops? Grab them even if you'd normally prefer a more powerful 5-drop. Need removal? Take it. Pack 3 is about having a functional, well-balanced deck, not just raw power.

## Card Evaluation Principles

### CAPS Theory

**Cards that Affect the Board State** - Prioritize cards that directly impact the game:

- **Creatures** - Creatures block and attack, directly affecting the board
- **Removal** - Kill spells, bounce effects, direct damage—these deal with threats
- **Combat Tricks** - Instant-speed effects that affect combat

Lower priority:

- **Card Draw** - You need draw, but it's not as high priority as board impact
- **Evasion Auras** - These are good but often narrow
- **Utility** - Situational effects

### Mana Curve Matters

Examine the distribution of mana values in your deck:

- You need to play something early (turns 1-3)
- You need to play something in the midgame (turns 4-5)
- You need some payoff cards for later (turns 6+)

A good rule: if you can't cast anything on turn 2 or 3, your deck is likely too slow and clunky.

### Cheap Cards Are More Valuable

When comparing two cards of similar power level, prefer the cheaper one. A 2-mana creature and a 5-mana creature might both be "good," but the 2-drop is almost always better because:

- You can cast it on turn 2
- It pressures your opponent earlier
- Cheap removal can answer threats at any stage of the game

### Mana Fixing in Draft

- If you're playing two colors, you likely don't need heavy fixing. Just pick basic mountains and forests.
- If you're splashing a third color (usually for one powerful card), you need fixing—but only if that fixing helps you cast your splash cards. Don't pick a green card to help you cast a black spell.
- Splash big, expensive cards, not cheap ones. You're more likely to have the mana for a 5-drop splash in a splash color than a 1-drop.

## Deck Building Guidelines

### Minimum Card Count

- Minimum: 40 cards (not including basic lands)
- You will usually draft 42-45 cards (depending on set and pack distribution)
- This means you'll draft 45 cards, play 23 nonlands + 17 lands = 40 total

### Land Ratios

- **Aggressive decks (lots of cheap creatures)**: 16 lands, 24 nonlands
- **Midrange decks (balanced power)**: 17 lands, 23 nonlands (standard)
- **Slow decks (expensive spells, card draw)**: 18 lands, 22 nonlands

### Color Distribution

If you're in two colors:

- Roughly 12-13 sources of the primary color
- Roughly 10-12 sources of the secondary color
- Basic lands are usually enough with minimal fixing

If you're in three colors:

- 10-11 sources of primary color
- 8-9 sources of secondary color
- 4-5 sources of splash color
- Fixing lands become crucial

### Creature/Spell Balance

Highly dependent on the format and archetype, but generally:

- **Aggressive decks**: 15-18 creatures, 5-8 noncreature spells
- **Midrange decks**: 13-15 creatures, 8-10 noncreature spells
- **Control decks**: 8-12 creatures, 11-15 noncreature spells (mostly removal and draw)

## Common Limited Archetypes

### Aggressive (Weenie)

- Focuses on cheap creatures (1-3 mana)
- Turns pressure on quickly
- Often runs combat tricks for evasion
- Wants to win by turn 5-6
- Removal is usually combat damage, not spells

### Midrange

- Mix of efficient creatures and removal
- Answers early threats, then plays powerful creatures
- Typically the most consistent strategy
- Wins with efficient creatures generating incremental advantage

### Control

- Few creatures, lots of removal and card draw
- Stabilizes against all-in aggressive strategies
- Draws extra cards to maximize mana usage
- Wins with powerful late-game cards or repeated removal

### Ramp

- Mana acceleration (Llanowar Elves effects)
- Big creatures or spells
- Wants to cast expensive spells ahead of schedule
- Often green-based

## Advanced Draft Concepts

### Signal Reading

- **Good signals**: You see quality cards of a color coming around pack after pack
- **Bad signals**: You see few cards of a color, suggesting others are drafting it
- **Signals are weak in pack 1**: Focus on power over signals early

### Hate Drafting

- Picking a card not for your deck, but to deny it to opponents
- Generally not recommended in most draft formats
- More relevant in small pods or when you know an opponent needs a specific card

### Mana Fixing Value

- Lands are some of the most valuable picks in pack 3
- Any land that fixes mana and provides information (dual lands) goes up in value late
- Basic lands are free; dual lands must be picked during the draft

### Sideboarding

- Your unplayed drafted cards form your sideboard
- Identify cards that are good in specific matchups
- After game 1, you can swap any cards between your main deck and sideboard (no one-for-one restriction)
- Can add or remove lands between games
